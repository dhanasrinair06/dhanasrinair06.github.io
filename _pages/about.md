Regulatory affairs, to me, is quieter than it sounds. It didn't announce itself as a career — it arrived in fragments, and I spent years learning to recognize them.

The first fragment came during my Master's in microbiology in Mumbai, five years into the subject, when I stumbled onto a chapter on pharmacopoeias and cosmetic regulations. I read it thoroughly, oddly absorbed. I didn't know what the feeling was yet.

The second happened during my first encounter with a medical device due to a personal health challenge, and the right diagnosis put me on the right path. I couldn't stop thinking about the opposite: how a misdiagnosis sends someone down a longer, harder road while their symptoms only deepen. Diagnostic devices are quiet game-changers. Somewhere in there, I knew I wanted to help patients keep the hope of another day. I just didn't yet know regulatory affairs was the door.

Then life bypassed the plan. I'd been eyeing a PhD when a biotech company in Mumbai, Advanced Enzyme Technologies, called with a Regulatory Affairs Trainee role. I said yes thinking I'd loop back to microbiology eventually. Life had other plans. Three years there taught me RA was the calling, and I went deeper with a Master's in Regulatory Affairs at Northeastern, concentrating in clinical research. My microbiology background gave me a deep understanding of biotech products, their manufacturing, and mode of action. That technical foundation — paired with a research mindset — shapes how I frame problems and map the path forward. Alongside my studies, I mentored undergrads and worked as a TA. Somewhere in the program, medical devices became the calling. After graduating, I interned at another biotech — this time on cannabis products, a brand-new field with microbial rules being written in real time — before joining Abbott Vascular in 2022 as an RA Specialist II, working global submissions so devices reach the patients waiting for them, on time.

Off the clock, I'm usually doodling, gardening, cooking a vegan meal, reading, or on a yoga mat.
